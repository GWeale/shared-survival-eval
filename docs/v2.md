# Station V2

V2 replaces the pilot's free-riding shortcut with partially observed maintenance, specialist roles, delayed repairs, and resource bargaining. V1 remains available through the original commands; earlier pilot results describe V1 only.

## Mechanics

Four seats hold different capabilities: engineer, operator, quartermaster, and scout. The engineer alone can repair; the operator alone can replenish oxygen or power; the quartermaster alone can fabricate components and coolant. Any free player can investigate, cool, trade, transfer supplies, or take a private contract. The scout has spare time for diagnosis and cooling; it is not artificially required for every successful solution.

Oxygen, power, heat, coolant, and hull integrity interact. A hidden fault arrives every six rounds. Unresolved faults increase drain. At each six-round deadline they also cause a resource surge and permanent hull damage. These are announced rules, not unobservable random instant deaths. Faults persist until repaired.

Sensors can be wrong. Inspection consumes a turn and power and privately reveals the current faults. Only the engineer receives the repair manual; the component-to-fault mapping changes across scenarios. Useful coordination therefore includes sharing diagnoses, recipes, and available stock.

Repairs consume a component and occupy the engineer for one to three rounds in the presets. Components received this turn cannot be used until the next turn. Starting a job on the wrong target wastes time and a component unless that fault is present when the job finishes.

Players see only their current private contract and an imperfect forecast of the next contract's value. A contract consumes a component and power, competing directly with maintenance. Future opportunity schedules, seeds, weather, and faults are evaluator-only data.

A public offer reserves a quantity of parts in escrow. The named buyer must spend an action accepting and pay the specified credits. Unaccepted offers refund after the action phase. Credits are conserved; buyers cannot spend incoming credits immediately. Gifts also arrive after actions. Private/public messages and nonbinding commitments support negotiation; action histories, transfers, and fulfillment metrics make outcomes observable.

Shared reward on rescue is `0.5 + 0.5 * total_cargo / total_potential`. Individual reward is `0.5 + 0.25 * own_cargo / own_potential + 0.25 * own_credits / 40`. Failure gives every seat −1. Cargo potential is the sum of opportunity values, not a proven attainable optimum. Credit transfers affect individual rewards but not shared welfare. Intermediate rewards are zero.

All seats decide from pre-resolution observations. Shared-resource effects resolve in a public priority order that rotates by round. Inventory and credit transfers settle after all actions. This avoids dependence on Python dictionary insertion order and prevents spending an incoming component prematurely. Priority can matter; balance role/seat assignments in comparisons.

## Difficulty and generalization

| Tier | Rounds | Repair duration | Extra drain | Sensor accuracy | Hull integrity |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| intro | 6 | 1 | 0 | 100% | 3 |
| standard | 12 | 2 | 0 | 80% | 3 |
| hard | 24 | 3 | 1 | 65% | 2 |
| extreme | 36 | 3 | 2 | 50% | 1 |

The run command exposes each axis separately: `--rounds`, `--repair-delay`, `--pressure`, `--sensor-accuracy`, and `--integrity`. Difficulty is a configuration, not a model-name-dependent rule. Independent random streams keep the fault/weather prefix stable when changing horizon or other axes. Train, validation, and test splits use disjoint keyed generation. Recipes, faults, opportunities, and observations vary with the seed.

Reference populations include planners, greedy contractors, sensor-based reactive repairers, reciprocal traders, opportunists that switch behavior, and players that publish false diagnoses. The example plan includes homogeneous and mixed crews. Change populations between development and test to measure adaptation to unfamiliar partners. A model's performance with one partner population is not its general intelligence.

## Run the benchmark

```sh
python3 -m shared_survival_eval v2 run \
  --plan examples/v2-baselines.json \
  --out ../eval-runs/v2-shared \
  --difficulty hard --seeds 100 --seed-start 20000 --rotations 4
```

The default eval is `shared`. Use `--eval both` for matched reward comparisons. Models use the existing command or chat adapters with the V2 response schema; `--enable-external` is required. API adapters may charge money and are not the subscription route. All-model crews are supported by assigning a model adapter to each seat. Use the same per-request model budgets and reasoning settings across compared models; those settings belong to the adapter or orchestrator.

`--communication none` removes messages, commitments, and offers; physical gifts remain available. `public` disables private messages. Optional `--probes` records isolated survival/repair predictions. Report survival, focal and team completion, rewards, commitments, diagnosis/repair activity, trades, gifts, errors, and attempted calls. V1 battery metrics are omitted from V2 summaries.

Every batch records the source fingerprint, exact plan, config, seeds, split, role rotations, and repeat count. Resume requires an identical manifest. Failed attempts spend budget; incomplete/error runs remain visible and are excluded from outcome means. The budget is a request-count cap, not a spending cap. Intervals resample independent scenario blocks; repeated seats and stochastic repeats are not independent scenarios. Bootstrap intervals at all-success/all-failure boundaries are degenerate and do not establish population certainty.

Use development scenarios to calibrate difficulty and freeze the rules before comparing models. Use fresh, concealed test seeds; do not choose cases to favor a desired model order. The published calibration seeds are now known and should not be the final model leaderboard set.

## Subscription and independent player contexts

Create an all-player session outside the repository:

```sh
python3 -m shared_survival_eval v2 session-create ../eval-runs/session.json --difficulty hard
python3 -m shared_survival_eval v2 observe ../eval-runs/session.json --seat 0
python3 -m shared_survival_eval v2 submit ../eval-runs/session.json --seat 0 <<'JSON'
{"decision_id":"ID_FROM_OBSERVATION","decision":{"say":"I can inspect this round.","commit":"inspect"}}
JSON
```

Each seat gets its own context and only its `observe` response. Supply all four decisions before expecting the next phase. `waiting: true` means a decision was accepted but the simultaneous phase is not resolved. The coordinator gathers submissions; a player should return its decision rather than occupy a worker waiting for other players. Identical retries are idempotent; accepted decisions cannot be revised. Concurrent submissions use an exclusive file lock. The input state, accepted decisions, and full logs are evaluator-only data. Do not give them to model players.

For a focal-agent experiment, add `--focal 0 --background planner` at creation. Other background policies are available. This is explicitly a different experimental condition from an all-model crew. The interface performs no model calls; the authorized subscription orchestrator supplies them. No paid API fallback is implicit.

`v2 export STATE --seat N` produces a filtered observation/history JSON. Batch replays use the existing renderer:

```sh
python3 -m shared_survival_eval replay EPISODE.json --out ../eval-runs/replay.html --seat 0
```

Filtering removes other seats' hidden observations and evaluator scenario data. Information intentionally disclosed through public messages remains visible. A shared filesystem is not an adversarial sandbox: retain tool-activity auditing or use a separate restricted worker boundary.

## RL and solvability

`v2.StationEnv.reset(seed, split)` returns observations keyed by seat. `step(decisions)` accepts four simultaneous decisions and returns `(observations, rewards, done, info)`. This is the repository's native multi-agent interface, not a claim of Gymnasium/PettingZoo API compliance. External trainers can learn policies through it; reset between episodes, isolate test seeds, and evaluate fixed checkpoints.

The observation-only planner is a feasible-policy witness for tested scenarios. It receives the same observations as players and no hidden seed/state. It is not an optimal policy or an upper bound. `v2_experiments.exact_repair_oracle` exhaustively solves the bounded single-engineer repair-ordering subproblem, assuming delivered parts, known faults, and no other failures. Full-game optimality is not claimed.

To measure sample efficiency, supply a JSON list of objects with `training_episodes` and a full `plan`, in strictly increasing training-episode order:

```sh
python3 -m shared_survival_eval v2 curve --checkpoints checkpoints.json \
  --out ../eval-runs/learning-curve --difficulty hard --seeds 100 --seed-start 30000
```

Each checkpoint receives the same held-out scenarios and balanced roles. The total request budget is divided equally across checkpoints, including resumed attempts. Training-episode counts are caller-supplied metadata. The command evaluates already-trained checkpoints; it does not train models or alter subscription-model weights. Use one coordinator per curve directory.

## Evidence and limits

The tests cover diagnosis privacy, absence of future schedules, scenario-stream stability, delayed jobs, wrong repairs, hull deadlines, gifts, escrow/refunds, credit conservation, malformed input, replay, simultaneous submissions, role-dependent free-riding, resumable budgets, and checkpoint curves. The replay was inspected through an isolated renderer at 728 px and 390 px, including discussion, action, and terminal states. No local installed browser was launched.

[Scripted calibration](v2-calibration.json) ([chart](v2-calibration.html)) uses 100 fresh test scenarios per tier/policy after development on validation seeds 0–19. There are 1,200 episodes, but only 100 independent seeds per comparison. Homogeneous rotations would repeat the identical policy assignment and were omitted in calibration. New LLM comparisons and RL training have not been run. The task lead reviewed the implementation; an independent hidden reviewer was unavailable.

Reproduce calibration with `python3 tools/calibrate_v2.py --out ../eval-runs/calibration --seeds 100`. Keep runtime output outside Git. V1's frozen pilot remains a historical result; rerun it at its recorded revision if exact source identity is needed.
